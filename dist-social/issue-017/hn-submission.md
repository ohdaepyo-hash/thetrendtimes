## First comment by the submitter

> The post is a reading of six primary sources, each with its own sample:
>
> - The Startup Project (https://startupproject.org/resources/state-of-solo-founders/): 970 startups from directory submissions, May to October 2026, 592 of them solo. 82% of solo founders reported no revenue, 2.5% (15) reported $1,000 or more a month. Self-reported and unverified.
> - ChartMogul (https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-startups-become-what-they-hunt/): 1,043 companies that reached $10k MRR by May 2023. Median annualized gross revenue retention at that point was 24.6% under $30/mo, 31.6% at $30 to $299 and 70.5% at $300 to $2,999.
> - ChartMogul (https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-growth-decay-report/): 700+ private software companies. Median growth endurance 43%; 18% held or raised their growth rate.
> - Growth Unhinged (https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-state-of-b2b-monetization-in-2026): 230+ B2B software and AI companies. Median target AI gross margin about 50%; hybrid pricing at 37%.
> - OpenMeter (https://openmeter.io/blog/monetization-benchmarks-2026): the same survey read for engineering. Fixed pricing expected to fall from 42% to 8% in three years.
> - Acquire.com (https://blog.acquire.com/business-acquisitions-2026-webinar/): webinar recap. SaaS at about 3-5x profit or 1-3x revenue.
>
> Caveats: none of the samples is random. ChartMogul's set is its own customers that survived three more years, the Growth Unhinged survey spans companies from under $1M to over $150M ARR, and OpenMeter and Acquire.com sell into the markets they describe. One thing I flagged in the post: ChartMogul's headline says $300+ companies grow "3-5x faster," but its listed averages (22% vs 2%, 4%, 5%) give roughly 4x to 11x. If you sell a product under $30 a month, what does your annual revenue retention look like?
