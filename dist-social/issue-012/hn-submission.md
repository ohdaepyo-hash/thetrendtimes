## First comment by the submitter

> Primary sources, since the post is mostly a reading of them:
>
> - Trust table: https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai/data/ai-trust (n = 14,304). 48.0% trust AI output when they can easily verify it, 16.3% for many tasks but not important decisions, 16.3% for low-risk tasks only, 12.3% not for most tasks, 6.6% including important decisions.
> - The overview page (https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/) says more than 87% had "some level of trust", up from 31% last year. The 87% is the sum of four conditional answers. The 2025 survey (https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/) asked about trust in accuracy on a scale and reported 33% trust against 46% distrust, so the two years are different questions.
> - Sentiment: https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai/data/ai-sentiment (n = 14,428). 31.6% very favorable plus 30.4% somewhat favorable. 2025 was 60%.
> - Use: coding assistants or agents 66%, chat tools 63%. Among daily users, 30.9% spend four or more hours a day with AI (https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai).
> - Policy: 30% say AI use is left to the individual, 24% say their organization publishes approved tools or guidance (press release, October 6, 2026).
>
> Caveats: self-reported, recruited through Stack Overflow's own site, blog and email list, 30,903 valid responses against 49,000+ last year. The press release and the survey pages disagree on a few percentages (usefulness, in-house tools); the post notes which one it uses.
