## First comment by the submitter

> Primary sources, since the post is mostly a reading of them:
>
> - Report: https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-commits-pushes-reach-new-highs-in-2026-as-developers-build-with-agents/ (window: October 1, 2025 to September 30, 2026, public and private repositories unless noted).
> - Agent-started pull requests: more than 40% of pull requests in September 2026, up from 17% in March; 110 million in September alone. The glossary counts only agents GitHub can detect, so the share is a floor.
> - Those pull requests change multiple files 87% of the time (79% without detected agents), and 90% merged within two weeks in September (about 86% for the comparison group). The report gives no defect or revert rates.
> - Model choice: 61% of Copilot users who selected a model themselves used more than one in September. Paid plans went from 29 distinct models in May to 47, and no single model carried more than 25% of requests.
> - HydraFusion (research preview, September 4, 2026): https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/project-hydrafusion-frontier-quality-via-multi-model-orchestration/. Offline, against Claude Opus 5: TerminalBench 2.1 +4.9 points at 67% lower cost, DeepSWE -1.5 points at 36% lower, CheckpointBench -0.1 points at 65% lower. The Octoverse report says it is now generally available as GitHub Copilot Fusion.
> - TypeScript is the primary language in 35% of 2026 repositories with an agent signal and 24% of those without.
>
> Caveat: this is GitHub's telemetry about GitHub's product, and the 2024 and 2025 comparison figures were recalculated on the new October-to-September window. If you track it, how does your revert rate for agent-started pull requests compare with the rest?
