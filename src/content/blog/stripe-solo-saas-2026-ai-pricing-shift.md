Kubrio has one employee. Its founder, Vlad Stan, runs a platform where kids aged 6 to 13 make films, games and comics, and a Grok agent called Stash reads his Stripe Treasury account every morning. Stash checks balances, new and canceled subscriptions, failed payments and refunds, then sends Stan a report ([Stripe Treasury for agents](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/treasury-for-agents)).

Stripe's 2026 data suggests that setup is becoming normal. In the second quarter of 2026, 63% of C corps formed through Stripe Atlas had a single founder, the highest share Stripe has recorded ([Atlas solo founder study](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits)).

If you sell software alone or with a small team, you have probably felt two pressures this year. Building got cheaper, so competitors multiply. Customers started asking why they pay per seat when an agent does part of the work. This piece reads six Stripe publications from May to October 2026 and pulls out what they measure on growth, pricing and retention, and where they stop. It ends with a checklist for this week and this month.

## What Stripe's 2026 Data Actually Measures

The six sources measure different things, so the numbers do not stack into one story. Two are research posts, three are blog posts on payments or survey data and one is a product announcement.

The survey is the softest data. Stripe asked 200 C-suite and director-level leaders at SaaS platforms in North America and Europe in March 2026. Half used Stripe, and the sample split evenly between vertical and horizontal platforms ([Stripe platform survey](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platform-trends-in-AI-era)). Those answers are plans and opinions, not billing records.

The Stripe SaaS Index is the hardest data. It is a weekly same-business index of pay-in volume at non-AI SaaS companies, covering an average of 72,000 businesses a week in 2026 ([Stripe SaaS Index post](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-saaspocalypse-was-more-like-a)). Stripe states its limits plainly. The index cannot see payments processed off Stripe, operating expenses or margins.

The solopreneur figures sit in between. Stripe Economics built a proxy index from about 115 solopreneur-focused platforms plus every solo Atlas business, and the authors say it "almost certainly understates" the real population ([The Age of the Solopreneur](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-age-of-the-solopreneur)). Treat it as a direction, not a census.

## New Platform Businesses Are Up More Than 180%

Platforms are software companies that let other businesses take payments through them, and Stripe Connect is the product they use for it. Stripe added more new platforms in the three months before September 17, 2026 than in the last six months of 2025. Over those three months, new platform businesses rose more than 180% year over year ([Stripe Connect post](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platforms-are-surging-despite-the-saaspocalypse)).

Stripe says it checked the new platforms and found real customer activity. Platforms that went live in January 2026 reached $1 million in payment volume at a higher rate than any earlier cohort over the same period ([Stripe Connect post](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platforms-are-surging-despite-the-saaspocalypse)).

AI shows up in how these platforms get built. As of August 2026, more than 55% of new Stripe integrations involved some form of AI assistance. The number of self-serve SaaS platforms building complete integrations rose roughly 360% year over year ([Stripe Connect post](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platforms-are-surging-despite-the-saaspocalypse)).

This growth came right after a scare. Starting in late January 2026, software companies lost roughly $1 trillion in market value in 30 days, a sell-off the press called the "SaaSpocalypse" ([Stripe Connect post](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platforms-are-surging-despite-the-saaspocalypse)). Stripe Economics puts the total at up to $2 trillion once the following weeks are included ([Stripe SaaS Index post](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-saaspocalypse-was-more-like-a)).

Revenue did not follow the stock prices. The SaaS Index's year-over-year growth is now above 30%, at or above the summer 2023 highs. From May to December 2025 the index grew 14%. In the same length of time since January 2026 it grew 21%, and it now sits 4% above its pre-sell-off trend ([Stripe SaaS Index post](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-saaspocalypse-was-more-like-a)).

## Solo Founders Now Form 63% of New Atlas C Corps

The Atlas study counts a solo founder as someone who incorporated through Stripe Atlas with no cofounders. Its 63% figure covers C corps formed so far in the second quarter of 2026, as of the May 28 post ([Atlas solo founder study](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits)). Stripe repeats it as a full-quarter figure in its October Treasury announcement ([Stripe Treasury for agents](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/treasury-for-agents)).

Higher earners are growing fastest. More than twice as many solopreneurs in Stripe's proxy index earned over $1 million in 2025 as in 2023, and close to three times as many crossed $5 million and $10 million. The share of solopreneurs above those thresholds also doubled in two years ([The Age of the Solopreneur](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-age-of-the-solopreneur)).

Stripe Economics also argues the surge is not a wave of empty filings. The US saw roughly 5.7 million new business applications in 2025, and Delaware incorporations have grown about 40% year over year since early 2025 ([The Age of the Solopreneur](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-age-of-the-solopreneur)). Delaware is where founders incorporate when they plan to raise money, so the authors read its growth as real intent.

One figure in that post is easy to misread. The share of businesses reaching $1 million in cumulative revenue within a year of going live on Stripe was about 30% higher for the 2025 cohort than for 2023, and about 3x higher than for 2019. The authors note that this counts all businesses, not only solopreneurs ([The Age of the Solopreneur](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-age-of-the-solopreneur)).

## The Gap Between Median and Top-Decile Solo Builders

More solo founders does not mean a typical solo founder is doing better. For Atlas solo startups, median revenue in the first six months fell 23% year over year in 2025, while top-decile revenue rose 19%. Four years ago the top decile made about 34 times the median. In 2025 it made 61 times ([Atlas solo founder study](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits)).

Stripe looked at thousands of solo Atlas startups formed in 2022 and 2023, each with at least two years of revenue. It compared the top decile with the middle decile and found five patterns ([Atlas solo founder study](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits)):
- **AI-native products.** Top-decile founders were about twice as likely to build products whose core depends on AI models. At 24 months, AI-native solo startups earned almost twice the revenue of others. The gap came from the 50th to 95th percentile, not from a few outliers.
- **Selling abroad early.** In month one, top founders sold into an average of 10 countries against three. By month 24 it was 40 non-US countries against six, and international sales were 51% of revenue against 2%.
- **B2B focus.** Top founders were nearly 30% more likely to sell to businesses. At month 24, median B2B solo revenue was more than four times median B2C revenue, and the pattern held for bootstrapped startups.
- **Retention.** Nearly 30% of first-month customers at top-decile startups came back the next month, against 8% in the middle decile. By the start of year two, those first customers spent 47% more than at first.
- **Teams versus solo.** At month 24, top-decile multifounder startups earned 53% more than top-decile solo founders. Among bootstrapped startups at the 99th percentile, the gap shrank to 5%.

Recurring billing may explain part of the retention gap. Top-decile B2B and B2C founders were 26 and 20 percentage points more likely to use a recurring billing model than their middle-decile peers ([Atlas solo founder study](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits)). In B2B, top founders kept first-month customers at six times the median rate.

## Seat Pricing Is Losing Ground to Usage and Agents

The platform survey shows pricing in motion. Of the 200 leaders, 44% plan to make multiple pricing changes in the next 12 months. The share expecting seats to be their primary pricing model falls from 16% in 2026 to 11% in 2027 ([Stripe platform survey](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platform-trends-in-AI-era)).

Horizontal platforms serve many industries, while vertical platforms serve one. By 2027, only 7% of horizontal platforms expect seats to stay primary, against 14% of vertical ones ([Stripe platform survey](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platform-trends-in-AI-era)). Horizontal tools grew by selling more seats to bigger customers, and agents now do work that used to need a person in a seat.

Few leaders plan to drop seats entirely. One in four plan to keep per-seat fees and charge separately for agent work through credits, usage or outcomes. Zendesk now charges for resolutions alongside its subscriptions. Another 7% plan to price agents like seats, with a base fee plus a license per agent ([Stripe platform survey](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platform-trends-in-AI-era)).

Revenue mix splits by customer size. Among horizontal platforms serving midsize and enterprise customers, 60% expect usage or hybrid pricing to lead revenue in 2027, and 3% expect embedded finance to lead. Among vertical platforms serving micro and small businesses, 34% expect embedded finance to lead and 22% expect usage or hybrid pricing ([Stripe platform survey](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platform-trends-in-AI-era)). Embedded finance here means payments, financing, financial accounts and card issuing inside the software.

## Agents in the Money Flow Change Retention Math

Platforms already use AI on themselves. Over the past 12 months, 81% used AI to speed up research and development, 79% added AI features to existing products and 58% are rebuilding workflows around agents. At Granum, agents helped support staff handle 23% more tickets and onboarding specialists serve 34% more customers ([Stripe platform survey](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platform-trends-in-AI-era)).

The worry is real too. Of the surveyed leaders, 57% fear losing customers to AI-driven competitors, and 45% say their AI investments have lowered operating costs ([Stripe platform survey](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platform-trends-in-AI-era)).

Stripe's answer to that worry is money movement. In the survey, 60% of platforms call embedded finance very important or mission-critical to retention over the next 24 months. More than half name payments data as a top competitive advantage. As of September 2026, the number of active platforms offering Stripe Capital nearly doubled year over year ([Stripe platform survey](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platform-trends-in-AI-era)).

The Connect post names three traits of hard-to-replace platforms. They run key workflows such as scheduling, they hold business data such as customer histories and they connect daily operations to money movement ([Stripe Connect post](https://stripe.com/blog/saas-platforms-are-surging-despite-the-saaspocalypse)). Advent partner Eric Noeth put it as a test: what breaks the day the customer turns the product off?

Treasury for agents pushes the same idea into agent workflows. Through Stripe's Model Context Protocol (MCP) server, agents in tools like ChatGPT and Claude can check balances, pay invoices, move funds between accounts and convert currencies. Card creation and agent spending limits are announced as coming soon. Businesses choose which accounts an agent can see, whether it can act and when a human must approve money movement ([Stripe Treasury for agents](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/treasury-for-agents)).

Agents also arrive as customers of Stripe itself. AI-influenced journeys now make up nearly 4x the share of Stripe sign-ups they did the January before. Stripe counts MCP use, its command-line interface (CLI), Claimable Sandboxes (launched October 2025) and referrals from LLMs ([The Age of the Solopreneur](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-age-of-the-solopreneur)). In the SaaS Index, young SaaS firms trailed mature ones on these signals by five points in early 2026, flipped in April and now lead by four ([Stripe SaaS Index post](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-saaspocalypse-was-more-like-a)).

## What the Numbers Do Not Say

Every figure here comes from Stripe, about businesses on Stripe. A company that bills through another processor is invisible to it. Stripe also sells the products these posts recommend, from Connect to Capital to Treasury.

The survey numbers are expectations for 2027, not results. The 63% solo share describes who incorporates through Atlas, not who earns money. And the top-decile traits are correlations. Stripe does not claim that selling abroad or adding AI causes the revenue gap.

The SaaS Index excludes AI companies by design, and it tracks gross pay-in volume, not profit ([Stripe SaaS Index post](https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-saaspocalypse-was-more-like-a)). A business can grow revenue there while its inference bill eats the margin. None of the six sources measures margins for solo founders.

## Practical Checklist for Solo Builders and Teams

### This week
- Write down your current pricing model in one line: seats only, seats plus usage or credits, or pure usage. Compare it with the 11% of platforms that expect seats to stay primary in 2027.
- Pull your first-month cohort from your billing data and count how many customers paid again in month two. The top-decile benchmark is nearly 30%, the middle decile 8%.
- Check whether an agent can reach your product at all through an MCP server, a CLI or a sandbox, and list which one you would ship first.

### This month
- If you run vertical SaaS, map where money already moves in your customers' workflow (payments, payouts, financing) and pick one point to own.
- If you sell B2B early, turn on multi-currency pricing and test sales outside your home market. Top-decile founders earned 51% of revenue abroad.
- Pick one internal workflow, such as support triage or onboarding, automate it with an agent and measure tickets or customers handled per person before and after.
